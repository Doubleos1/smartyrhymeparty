Candidate for President

Dr. Kolby Yang

Fictional profile for a class project

Dr. Yang has worked as a nurse, a doctor and a business leader, so he understands healthcare, money and people. He knows how to run big organizations, listen to workers and fix what is broken. He has spent his career getting people with different views to sign the same plan, which is what the country needs after years of gridlock.

About Kolby

Kolby Yang grew up in St. Paul, Minnesota, in a working-class immigrant family. He earned a double-major degree at the University of Pennsylvania, in business from the Wharton School and in nursing, and worked as a nurse while he continued his studies. He then earned his M.D. at Harvard Medical School and a Master of Public Health at Yale. As a family-medicine physician he ran a network of rural clinics in Minnesota as chief executive, led a telehealth program that brought doctors to small towns, and served on his state’s board of health, where he helped write the state’s rural health plan.

Our platform, in order of importance

Our philosophy: use what works, wherever the idea comes from. We want solutions that are tough, fair and affordable.

1

Deportation and Immigration

“Secure, Fair, Practical.”
“Keep it secure, fair, and clear, practical rules for everyone here!”

Our stance

We enforce the law and we treat people fairly. Anyone who is a danger to the public, such as a person convicted of a serious violent crime, is removed first. People who came as children, or who have lived here for years, worked, paid taxes and have no serious record, can earn legal status by passing a background check, paying a fine and back taxes, and learning English over several years. We will not deport Dreamers.

What we would do

Hire more immigration judges to clear the court backlog, put money into border technology and ports of entry, and require employers to check work eligibility. We will expand legal work visas so farms and hospitals can hire legally.

2

Education

“Every Kid Gets a Real Shot.”
“Give every kid a real shot, so they can reach what they’ve sought!”

Our stance

Great public schools are the best investment America can make, and schools must be held accountable for results.

What we would do

Raise teacher pay in high-need schools, fund small-group tutoring for reading and math, expand career and technical classes and paid apprenticeships so students can graduate with a job path, and make community college tuition-free for high-demand jobs like nursing, trades and technology. We will also simplify the FAFSA and make student loan repayment tied to income.

3

Healthcare

“Keep Your Plan, Add a Choice.”
“Keep your plan, add a choice, in your care you get a voice!”

Our stance

Americans should not go bankrupt from getting sick, and the government should not take over all of healthcare. We keep private insurance and add a public option, a government-run plan that competes with private plans.

What we would do

Offer the public option on the insurance marketplace, require hospitals and insurers to post clear prices, let Medicare negotiate more drug prices, cap insulin costs, expand telehealth for rural areas, and cover mental healthcare the same as physical care.

4

Environment

“Clean Air, Smart Markets.”
“Clean air and smart markets too, a greener future made for you!”

Our stance

Climate change is real, and the cheapest way to fix it is to use the power of the market instead of a thousand new rules.

What we would do

Put a steady price on carbon pollution and send the money back to households as a dividend, give tax credits for every clean energy source (solar, wind, geothermal, hydro and nuclear), and spend more on wildfire prevention and forest thinning, which matters a lot in Oregon. Companies choose how to cut pollution, but they must meet enforceable limits.

5

Food Shortage

“Full Plates, Full Fields.”
“Full plates and full fields to share, healthy food for everywhere!”

Our stance

Nobody in America should go hungry, and farmers must be able to stay in business. Oregon Food Bank reports that 1 in 7 people and 1 in 6 kids in Oregon and Southwest Washington face food insecurity, and food bank visits reached a record 2.9 million last year.

What we would do

Protect SNAP and school meals for families with children while linking adults to job training, modernize the H-2A farm worker visa so farms can get workers on time, fund farm-to-school programs and local food hubs, and cut food waste with tax breaks for donated food.

Campaign plan: Oregon first

Oregon is our home base and launch state.

Why Oregon

Oregon has 8 electoral votes, and it gives all 8 to whoever wins the state. In 2024, Harris won Oregon 55.3% to 41.0%, so the major parties treat it as safe and rarely campaign there. That makes ads and events cheap and our message easier to hear.

Most important, Oregon’s biggest voting group is nonaffiliated voters.

Plan of attack inside Oregon

Phase 1: LaunchWillamette Valley
Portland area for the most voters, Salem and Marion County for farms (Marion has Oregon’s highest farm sales) and state government, and Eugene for college students.
Phase 2: BuildCentral and Southern Oregon
Jackson County voted for Trump by about 6.6 points in 2024. This is where we introduce ourselves as the practical alternative.
Phase 3: FinishEastern Oregon
Including Umatilla County, where about 52% of voters are independents (the highest share of any Oregon county with at least 50,000 voters) and farming is a big part of the economy.
Little timeTiny rural counties
Reached with mail, radio and our social media page.

Beyond Oregon: we go next to the Pacific Northwest and to the Upper Midwest (Dr. Yang’s home), and spend little time in states that are locked in for one major party. Our realistic goal is to win states with many independents and keep any one candidate from reaching the 270 electoral votes needed, which sends the decision to Congress.

Three campaign events

Event 1

College town halls

University of Oregon, Oregon State and Portland State. They reach young nonaffiliated voters, and we set up voter-registration tables because Oregon’s registration deadline is 21 days before Election Day.

Event 2

Farm and food tours

Visits with Willamette Valley farmers and farmers markets. They show our food and farm labor plan, and they build the relationship with our farm funders.

Event 3

Ballot Drop Parties

Oregon votes entirely by mail, with ballots arriving about 14 to 18 days before the election. We host food-truck rallies next to ballot drop boxes in the last two weeks so supporters turn their ballots in on time.

Our best demographic group

Nonaffiliated voters, especially ages 18 to 34

They are Oregon’s largest group, and according to testimony submitted to the Oregon Legislature, more than half of voters ages 18 to 34 are nonaffiliated. Oregon’s primaries are closed, so these voters cannot vote in the Democratic or Republican primaries. Our pitch: “We are the party that wants you.”

How we get them to vote: registration tables, ballot-return events, text reminders, and funny rhyme-based videos for social media.

Everyone else: Democrats hear about the public option and clean energy. Republicans hear about secure borders, farm labor and fewer wasteful rules. We do not ignore anyone, but we spend the least time on voters who always vote straight party.

Who funds us

Two interest groups

American Farm Bureau Federation and Oregon Farm Bureau. They support farm labor reform, such as the Securing Agriculture’s Workforce Act of 2026, which would let workers stay up to 350 days and cap federal fees. They would back us because our immigration and food platform fixes their worker shortage. We could benefit them by modernizing H-2A and lowering labor costs for farms.

Environmental Defense Fund (EDF). This nonprofit is known for market-based solutions, including putting a price on carbon. They would back us because our environment plan uses markets and a carbon price. We could benefit them by passing a carbon price and clean energy credits.

Together they show a coalition of farmers and environmentalists that the old parties usually keep apart.
